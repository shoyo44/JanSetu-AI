JUDGMENT / ORDER

1. Leave granted. This appeal arises against the final judgment and order passed by the High Court of Delhi dismissing the regular bail application filed by the appellant under Section 439 CrPC in FIR No. 248/2023 registered for offences punishable under Section 420 and Section 406 of IPC.

2. FACTS OF THE CASE:
The complainant alleged that the appellant entered into a commercial agreement for supply of medical equipment and accepted an advance sum of Rs. 45,00,000/-. It is alleged that the appellant failed to deliver the consignments and misappropriated the funds. The appellant was arrested on November 12, 2023 and has remained in judicial custody for over nine months. The investigation is complete and the chargesheet has already been filed before the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate.

3. RATIO DECIDENDI (BINDING LEGAL PRINCIPLE):
Personal liberty guaranteed under Article 21 of the Constitution of India is the rule, and incarceration during the pendency of trial is the exception. Pre-trial detention cannot be transformed into punitive punishment. In commercial transactions giving rise to allegations of cheating under Section 420 IPC (or Section 318 BNS), where the entire investigation stands concluded, the chargesheet is submitted, and relevant documents are in judicial custody, continued custody of the accused serves no investigative purpose. Prolonged pre-trial detention without commencement of trial violates the constitutional mandate of speedy justice under Article 21. Bail cannot be withheld merely as a measure of pre-trial punishment.

4. OBITER DICTA (JUDICIAL OBSERVATIONS):
The Court observed that investigating agencies must exercise restraint in invoking criminal machinery to resolve commercial and civil contractual disputes. Where dispute resolution through civil remedy is available, criminalizing civil default burdens the trial courts and compromises undertrial personal liberties. Trial courts and High Courts must actively enforce the safeguards established in Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar and Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI.

5. DISPOSITION:
Appeal is allowed. The impugned order of the High Court is set aside. The appellant is directed to be released on bail under Section 439 CrPC (corresponding to Section 483 BNSS) subject to furnishing a personal bond in the sum of Rs. 50,000/- with two solvent sureties of like amount to the satisfaction of the trial court, and subject to the condition of surrendering his passport.
